# Build notes: The Internet in One Word

## The entry

The entry is the single word unhinged, eight characters, written to the
output file by a small Python program with no trailing newline.

## How the word was chosen

Phase one looked at the judge first. The judge is briefed as a comedy
editor who has read the entire internet, and it weighs five things in
order: the entry must be a single word describing the whole internet,
it must earn a laugh on its own, it must say something true about the
internet as a whole rather than one app or mood, it must surprise, and
it must land in one beat, spelled correctly and instantly readable.

The cliche radar ruled out the words most entrants will reach for
first: chaos, endless, addictive, memes, wild, everything, and the
long behaviour words such as doomscrolling and procrastination. Those
are either the first obvious answer or a description of what people do
on the internet rather than what the internet is.

Phase two built three contrasting candidates. The first was
procrastination, the sharp functional truth: the internet as the machine
for putting things off. The second was unhinged, the contrarian angle:
the internet as a place that has come off its hinges, funny because it
is true of the whole place at once. The third was scrollhole, a coined
word fusing scroll and rabbit hole, the high-craft fusion candidate.

Phase three scored each candidate out of ten on four measures: the
opening hook, originality, fit with the brief, and clarity of
execution. Procrastination scored twenty six out of forty: true and
clear, but predictable and long, so it lands slowly. Scrollhole scored
thirty one: the most original and surprising, but as a coined word it
needs half a beat to parse, and it describes a behaviour more than the
place itself. Unhinged scored thirty two: a strong hook, genuinely
funny on its own, true of the whole internet, surprising enough to
stand out from the obvious answers, and only eight letters, so it lands
in one beat. It also helps on ties, since the rules break exact ties
toward the smaller text.

## Verification

Two fresh runs of the program were compared and produced byte-identical
output. The self-gate in the program checks the entry is one word of
one to thirty characters, matching the bounty gate. The kit check was
run and its result is recorded in the build report.
